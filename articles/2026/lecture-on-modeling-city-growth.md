Yesterday, I gave lecture "What makes human behavior interesting to
physicists?" (lt. "Kuo fizikams įdomus žmonių elgesys?") to a large group of
high school students participating in a project "[Student for a
day](https://studentasvienaidienai.vu.lt/en)", organized by [Vilnius
University](https://www.vu.lt).

![first slide]({static}/uploads/2026/lecture-on-modeling-city-growth.jpg)

While I do not teaching anything directly related to my research, I have
volunteered to do a special introductory lecture and try to explain why
physicists are interested in social systems. Initially, I intended to tell
students more-or-less about the same things I did when [giving a
talk]({filename}/articles/2026/lecture-on-power-law-distributions.md) at
[Nacionalinė moksleivių akademija](https://nmakademija.lt). Yet instead of
simply trimming the lecture, I have decided instead to focus on a single
example and develop it a bit more. So, in the end I have talked about how
"normal" (Gaussian) humans create cities that are rather "peculiar". This
lead me to talk about Malthus and [Verhulst](/tag/verhulst-model/) models of
population growth (dedicated blog post should be published soon) and then
pivot to [preferential attachment](/tag/preferential-attachment/) as seen in
[Simon model](/tag/simon-model/) (I hope to prepare dedicated blog post
early next year).

If you are interested, you can take a sneak peek at my slides
[here]({static}/uploads/2026/Kononovicius2026SVD.pdf) (though they are in
Lithuanian).
